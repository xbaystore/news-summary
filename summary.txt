Wednesday, 11 November 2026 — the day in brief.

In Abu Dhabi, Russia and Ukraine sat down for their first US-mediated peace talks in nine months. Nearly four hours of closed-door discussion ended without a breakthrough, but both sides stayed at the table and a second day was scheduled — the most serious diplomatic movement in the nearly five-year war in months.

In Washington, the midterm picture finally settled: Democrats secured both the House and the Senate, reshaping the balance of power for the rest of the Trump presidency. President Trump, meanwhile, declared the war with Iran "effectively over" even as the US maritime blockade continued, and oil slipped to two-month lows. US inflation came in hotter than expected, consumer prices up 0.4% in October, reviving talk of a December Federal Reserve rate hike.

In Antalya, Türkiye, the COP31 climate summit wrestled with its central contradiction: a push to electrify 35% of the global economy by 2035, championed by fossil-fuel-reliant hosts, while the WMO confirmed 2026 is on track to be among the hottest years ever recorded and poorer nations pressed the rich world on climate finance.

Technology strained under its own success: TSMC reported insatiable demand for AI chips, Anthropic filed to go public on the Nasdaq, and Nvidia moved to investigate espionage allegations around its CEO. Elsewhere, Salvadoran troops struck a gang headquarters in Haiti, shutdown violence in Bangladesh killed a bus driver, and Delhi's toxic air eased slightly, its AQI down to 299 — still far from safe to breathe.
